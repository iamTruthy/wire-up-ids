Hardening via adversarial training, and an adaptive follow-up attack
====================================================================

Method
  Adversarial training. Padded copies of the TRAINING-set attack flows were
  generated with the same realizable packet-padding used in the evasion, kept
  their ATTACK label, and added to the training data. A new Random Forest was
  trained on the augmented set (2,264,594 original + 446,117 padded flows).

Before and after (padded-attack evasion, full test set, all attack types)
  BEFORE hardening: 0.4345
  AFTER  hardening: 0.0000
  Hardened model recall on NORMAL (unpadded) attacks: 0.9978
  Hardened model specificity on benign traffic:       0.9993

  Adversarial training closed the padding evasion completely with no
  measurable loss of normal-attack detection or benign specificity. The
  model learned that a padded port scan is still a port scan.

Adaptive follow-up: attack a feature family NOT trained against
  The hardened model detects the 1000 target port scans at 1.0000 baseline.
  Perturbing TIMING features (flow duration, IAT statistics), which were not
  part of the padding hardening:
    perturb 1 timing feature  -> 0.0000
    perturb 3                 -> 0.0010
    perturb 5                 -> 0.0020
    perturb 7                 -> 0.0870

  The timing attack reaches only 8.7% at 7 features, far below the ~93% that
  padding achieved against the original model. Two likely reasons: timing
  features carry less port-scan signal than size features, and adversarial
  training may have broadened the model's reliance across more features. The
  curve is still rising at k=7 and had not plateaued.

Conclusion
  Adversarial training raised the cost of evasion substantially and even
  generalized partly to an untrained perturbation family. It did not make the
  model immune. A determined adaptive attacker still finds headroom, and a
  combined size-and-timing perturbation would likely do better. Robustness
  here is an arms race in which the defender raises the bar, not a solved
  problem.
